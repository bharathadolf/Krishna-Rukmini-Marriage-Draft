# 🛠️ BUILD.md — The Screenwriter's Self-Interrogation Document
### *Krishna & Rukmini — A Feature Film (2 hrs 30 min)*
> **How to use this file:**
> This is your creative mirror. Answer every question honestly, in writing, before you lock any structural or scripting decision. Some questions will be easy. The hard ones are the ones that matter. Return to this file at every major draft milestone.

---

## SECTION 01 — PREMISE & THEME STATEMENT
*The foundational layer. If this is unclear, nothing else will work.*

### 01-A. The Core Premise
- [ ] In one sentence — what is this film ABOUT? *(Not the plot. The meaning.)*
- [ ] What is the central **emotional truth** this film wants to prove to the audience?
- [ ] If someone walked out of the theatre and described this film to a friend in one line — what would that line be? Is that the line I want?
- [ ] What is the film's **argument**? What does it say about love, duty, fate, or devotion that is *true and universal*?
- [ ] Is the premise **active**? Does it contain a character, a want, and an obstacle? *(e.g., "A woman who must…" not "A story about…")*
- [ ] Does the premise work **without the mythology**? Would this story function if Krishna and Rukmini were two modern people? If yes, what does that tell me about the story's core?
- [ ] What is the premise's **emotional hook**? Why should an audience member who has never heard of Bhagavata Purana care deeply about this story?
- [ ] Does the premise place Rukmini as an **agent** of the story — or a passive receiver? Which do I want?
- [ ] Does the premise place Krishna as a **divine figure**, a **hero**, or a **man in love** — or all three? Can the story hold all three simultaneously?

---

### 01-B. The Theme Statement
- [ ] What is the film's **controlling theme** — the one sentence that, if spoken aloud, would be the moral of the story? *(e.g., "True devotion conquers all political power.")*
- [ ] What is the **counter-theme** — the argument the antagonists (Rukmi, Shishupala, Jarasandha) represent? *(e.g., "Power and alliance are stronger than personal choice.")*
- [ ] Does the story dramatise a **collision** between the theme and counter-theme in every major scene?
- [ ] Is the theme **earned** or **stated**? Does the audience feel it — or is it told to them through dialogue?
- [ ] Is the theme **specific to this story** — or is it so universal that it could belong to any romance film?
- [ ] What does this story say about **free will vs. destiny**? How do I dramatise that tension without making the answer feel predetermined?
- [ ] What does the film say about **devotion (bhakti)** that is different from what every other devotional film has said before?
- [ ] What is the theme's **emotional cost**? What must the protagonist sacrifice or endure for the theme to feel true?
- [ ] By the END of the film — has the theme been **proved or questioned**? Is it a triumphant statement or a complex, earned truth?

---

### 01-C. The Story Problem (Current Creative Problem to Solve)
- [ ] **The Mythology Problem:** The audience already knows the ending. How do I make the journey feel uncertain and alive?
- [ ] **The Divinity Problem:** If Krishna is God — can he fail? If he cannot fail, where is the dramatic tension?
- [ ] **The Passivity Problem:** Does Rukmini have enough active scenes, or is she primarily waiting and hoping?
- [ ] **The Villain Problem:** Rukmi, Shishupala, and Jarasandha are the primary antagonists — but which of them is the *true* dramatic antagonist? Is it the external force (the army), the political force (Rukmi's ambition), or the internal force (Rukmini's fear)?
- [ ] **The Scale Problem:** This story operates at two scales simultaneously — cosmic/divine AND personal/human. Am I balancing both, or does one overwhelm the other?
- [ ] **The Letter Problem:** Rukmini's 7-sloka letter is the emotional centrepiece of the entire story. How do I dramatise a *letter* for a cinematic audience without it becoming a monologue?
- [ ] **The Time Problem:** The story spans multiple kingdoms and timelines. How do I control exposition without front-loading the first act with information?
- [ ] **The Messenger Problem:** Sudeva the Brahmin is a crucial structural character. What is his emotional investment in this mission? Why does *he* matter?
- [ ] **The Brotherhood Problem:** Balarama's reluctance and eventual support is a critical character beat. Am I dramatising it fully or summarising it?
- [ ] **The Chase Problem:** The battle/chase sequence in Act 3 must be both cinematic and emotionally meaningful — not just spectacle. What is the *inner conflict* running under the exterior battle?
- [ ] **The Wedding Problem:** The film's climax is a wedding. How do I make a wedding feel like the resolution of a war, not a reward at the end of a quest?
- [ ] **The Father Problem:** King Bhishmaka is a man crushed between his children. What is his arc? Does he get a resolution, or does his tragedy remain open?
- [ ] **The Cosmic Opening Problem:** If I use the Ksheera Sagara (cosmic ocean) prologue — how long is too long? At what point does it become mythology lecture rather than cinematic hook?

## SECTION 02 — CHARACTER QUESTIONS
*Every story problem is a character problem in disguise.*

### 02-A. Rukmini
- [ ] What does Rukmini **want** in Act 1? *(External goal: to avoid the forced marriage)*
- [ ] What does Rukmini **need** emotionally? *(Internal need: to claim her own identity, sovereign choice)*
- [ ] What is Rukmini's **wound**? What has shaped her before the story begins?
- [ ] What is Rukmini's **greatest fear** — and does the story force her to face it directly?
- [ ] What is the single most **courageous** thing Rukmini does in this film? Is it the letter — or something more?
- [ ] Does Rukmini have a **character arc** — does she change — or does she remain constant and the world changes around her? Both are valid. Which am I writing?
- [ ] What is Rukmini's **voice**? How does she speak differently from every other character?
- [ ] What would Rukmini do if Krishna *did not come*? Does she have a backup plan? Does she need one?
- [ ] In the **temple scene** — what is Rukmini's internal state? What is she praying for, beneath the surface?

### 02-B. Krishna
- [ ] How do I show Krishna's **divinity** without making him feel *untouchable* or narratively inert?
- [ ] What is Krishna's **emotional stake** in this story? Is it love? Duty? Cosmic obligation? Which one is *primary*?
- [ ] Does Krishna have a **moment of doubt** or **internal conflict**? If not — why should the audience be worried for him?
- [ ] What is Krishna's **relationship to Balarama** as a *dramatic engine*, not just as an alliance?
- [ ] How does Krishna feel about the fact that Rukmini *chose* him before he chose her? Does that matter to him?
- [ ] Is Krishna reactive to Rukmini's letter — or does he already know? How does the film handle his *omniscience*?

### 02-C. The Antagonists
- [ ] What does **Rukmi** genuinely believe he is doing for Rukmini? What is his self-justification? Is he the villain, or a man who is simply wrong?
- [ ] What is **Shishupala's** specific hatred of Krishna? Is it jealousy, political rivalry, wounded pride — or all three?
- [ ] Is **Jarasandha** a character the audience understands — or is he simply a force of threat?
- [ ] Who among the antagonists has the **most screen time**, and is that the right choice?
- [ ] What moment reveals the antagonist's **humanity** — even if brief?

### 02-D. Supporting Characters
- [ ] What is **Sudeva the Brahmin's** inner motivation for accepting this dangerous mission?
- [ ] What is **Balarama's** actual objection to the mission — and what changes his mind?
- [ ] What is **Daruka the Charioteer's** role beyond logistics? Does he have a scene that is *his*?
- [ ] What is the emotional function of **Rukmini's Four Brothers** — do they serve as dramatic counterweights to Rukmi's authority, or are they incidental?
- [ ] Does **King Bhishmaka** have a scene that is purely his — where his tragedy is felt without interruption?

## SECTION 03 — STRUCTURAL QUESTIONS
*Structure is the spine. These questions test its integrity.*

- [ ] What is the **Inciting Incident** — the moment that breaks the status quo and forces the story into motion?
- [ ] What is the **Act 1 Break** — the point of no return that locks Rukmini (and the audience) into the story?
- [ ] What is the **Midpoint** — the reversal that redefines the stakes at the film's centre?
- [ ] What is the **Act 2 Break** — the darkest moment, when all seems lost?
- [ ] What is the **Climax** — the scene where the theme is decided and the outcome is determined?
- [ ] Does every scene in the film advance EITHER the plot OR the character — ideally both?
- [ ] What are the **three most important scenes** in the film? Are they getting the most attention in development?
- [ ] Is the **first ten minutes** doing its job: establishing the world, the protagonist, the tone, and the question the film will answer?
- [ ] Is the **last ten minutes** earning its emotional payoff — or is it rushed?
- [ ] For a **150-minute runtime** — how are the minutes distributed across acts? Is Act 2 strong enough to sustain the film's centre?
- [ ] What scene marks the **end of hope** for Rukmini — the moment before the rescue when everything seems to have failed?
- [ ] Does the film have a **false resolution** — a moment where the audience thinks it's over, before the real climax?

## SECTION 04 — WORLD & MYTHOLOGY QUESTIONS
*The mythological layer must be cinematic, not academic.*

- [ ] How much **prior knowledge** of Krishna mythology am I assuming from the audience?
- [ ] How will I handle the **Ksheera Sagara Madhanam** (cosmic ocean) origin sequence — flashback, prologue, visual memory, or narration?
- [ ] What is the **visual identity** of each kingdom (Dwarka, Vidarbha, Chedi, Magadha)? How do they look and feel distinctly different?
- [ ] How will the film distinguish between **the divine world** and **the mortal world** visually and tonally?
- [ ] What **real historical/cultural details** from Dwapara Yuga will I include to ground the world?
- [ ] Is the **Ambika Temple sequence** treated as a spiritual scene, a political scene, or both? Which choice serves the story better?
- [ ] What mythological elements will I **compress, expand, or omit** — and why?
- [ ] How do I prevent the film from becoming a **retelling** rather than an **original cinematic experience**?

---

## SECTION 05 — TONE & GENRE QUESTIONS
*Tone is the emotional contract with the audience.*

- [ ] What is the **single emotional experience** I want the audience to have when they walk out?
- [ ] Is this film **devotional** (designed to inspire faith), **dramatic** (designed to explore character), or **epic** (designed to overwhelm with scale)? Which is primary?
- [ ] Where does the film sit on the **realism spectrum** — fully mythological spectacle, or emotionally grounded and intimate?
- [ ] What is the **tonal baseline** of the film — do the early scenes feel intimate before the scale opens up?
- [ ] What comparable films define the **tone template**: *Baahubali, Jodhaa Akbar, RRR, Sita Ramam, Adipurush, Ben-Hur, Lawrence of Arabia*? Which elements from which film?
- [ ] Is the humour — if any — appropriate to the world and tone of this story?
- [ ] What is the **tonal shift** that marks the move from Act 1 to Act 2? Is it visible to the audience?

---

## SECTION 06 — AUDIENCE & EXPERIENCE QUESTIONS
*The audience is the final collaborator.*

- [ ] Who is the **primary audience** for this film — devotional audience, general cinema audience, or both?
- [ ] What does the audience **know** when they enter the theatre — and how does the film use or subvert that knowledge?
- [ ] At what moment in the film should the audience **first feel fear** for the protagonist?
- [ ] At what moment should the audience **first feel tears** threatening?
- [ ] At what moment should the audience **stand up** — even internally — in triumph?
- [ ] Is there a scene designed to provoke **silence** — a quiet, devastating emotional beat?
- [ ] What scene do I want the audience to **talk about for days** after the film?
- [ ] What moment is designed to make the audience feel **complicit in the story** — not just watching, but *inside* it?
- [ ] For an international audience unfamiliar with the mythology — does the film work as a **pure story** on its own terms?

## SECTION 07 — PARALLEL STORYTELLING QUESTIONS
*(Specific to the structural framework built in `05.Parallels/`)*

- [ ] Of the **seven parallel models** identified, which two or three will form the spine of the screenplay?
- [ ] How will I **transition between parallel strands** — hard chapter cuts, thematic cross-cutting, or visual motif bridges?
- [ ] Does the **Cosmic Parallel** (Ksheera Sagara to mortal world) have a clear visual throughline, or does it remain abstract?
- [ ] Does the **Psychological Parallel** (Rukmini's inner life) have dedicated screen time — or is it only expressed through action?
- [ ] Does the **Brotherhood Parallel** (Krishna/Balarama vs. Rukmini/Brothers) create genuine dramatic irony, or is it merely a structural device?
- [ ] How does the **Socio-Political Parallel** (divine love vs. imperial power) serve the story's theme — not just its plot?
- [ ] In the **Grand Synthesis** beat sheet — which sequence feels weakest? Which needs the most development?
- [ ] Does the parallel structure make the story feel **richer** — or does it make it feel **fragmented**?
- [ ] Is the parallel architecture **visible** to the audience — or does it operate beneath the surface as pure structure?

## SECTION 08 — DIRECTOR VISION QUESTIONS
*(For the `04.What_If_Directors/` framework)*

- [ ] What is the **core visual metaphor** of this film? *(e.g., water as love, fire as duty, darkness as political corruption)*
- [ ] What does **each key scene look like** — what is the camera doing, where is the light?
- [ ] Is the film primarily a **wide-lens epic** (landscapes, armies, scale) or a **close-lens intimate** film (faces, hands, breath)?
- [ ] What is the **signature image** of this film — the single frame that would represent it on a poster?
- [ ] What is the **sound identity** of this film — traditional Carnatic instruments, orchestral score, or a fusion?
- [ ] Where does the **silence** live in this film? What scenes need NO music?
- [ ] What is the **colour language** of the film — and does it shift across acts?
- [ ] In the **Michael Bay version** — which sequences would be expanded? Does expanding spectacle weaken the emotional core?
- [ ] In the **Zack Snyder version** — does the mythology and slow-motion treatment serve devotion, or does it aestheticise it into emptiness?
- [ ] What is the **version** of this film that *I* would direct — if it were mine alone?

---

## SECTION 09 — DIALOGUE & LANGUAGE QUESTIONS
*Dialogue is character. Every word must earn its place.*

- [ ] Does every character's **dialogue** sound uniquely different from every other character?
- [ ] Is Rukmini's **7-sloka letter** being treated as a dramatic monologue, a voiceover, a written sequence, or something else — and why?
- [ ] What **Sanskrit or classical references** will be woven into the dialogue — and how do I make them accessible without a footnote?
- [ ] Which scenes should have **no dialogue** — only action and score?
- [ ] Does any character explain the theme **directly in dialogue**? If yes — can that scene be rewritten to *show* the theme instead?
- [ ] Does the dialogue feel like it comes from **Dwapara Yuga** — or does it feel like a modern script in costume?

## SECTION 10 — THEME TEST QUESTIONS
*These are the hard questions. Answer them last — or first.*

- [ ] If I could only keep ONE scene from this entire film, which scene would I keep — and why? What does that tell me about the film's real centre?
- [ ] What is the scene I am most **afraid to write**? Why? That scene is probably the most important one.
- [ ] What does this film say that **has never been said before** about Krishna and Rukmini — or about love, duty, and choice?
- [ ] If I removed all the mythology and told this as a contemporary story — what remains? What is irreducibly *itself*?
- [ ] Am I writing this story because I **love it** — or because I think it will succeed? Which one is true at this moment?
- [ ] What is the film's **unanswered question** — the thing it leaves the audience thinking about long after the credits?
- [ ] What would **disappoint me most** about this film, if I saw the final product? Am I already making that mistake?

---

## SECTION 11 — PRODUCTION AWARENESS QUESTIONS
*The screenplay exists in the real world.*

- [ ] Is this screenplay **producible at the scale I am imagining** — or am I writing something that requires unlimited resources?
- [ ] What are the **five most expensive sequences** in this screenplay? Are all five essential — or can any be achieved more cleverly?
- [ ] What is the **core location architecture** — how many distinct practical locations does this film require?
- [ ] Are there scenes that can be combined or collapsed to serve the same emotional purpose with fewer resources?
- [ ] If this screenplay were made on half the intended budget — what would I *protect* at all costs, and what would I sacrifice?

---

## SECTION 12 — MILESTONE CHECKPOINTS
*Return to these questions at each draft milestone.*

| Milestone | Key Question to Answer |
|-----------|------------------------|
| **Before Scene 1** | Can I state the premise and theme in a single sentence each? |
| **End of Act 1 Draft** | Has the world been established and the conflict locked in without over-explanation? |
| **Midpoint of Draft** | Is Rukmini's active agency present in every major sequence? |
| **End of Act 2 Draft** | Is the darkest moment truly *dark enough* — or have I protected the characters too much? |
| **Completed First Draft** | Does the last scene echo the first scene in theme, not in plot? |
| **After Table Read** | Which scene made the readers laugh, cry, or go silent — and was that the *intended* scene? |
| **Final Draft Lock** | Can I cut 10 pages without losing anything essential? If not, what is padded? |

---

## The Single Most Important Question
*Return to this every time you sit down to write.*

> **"Does this scene — right now — make the audience feel something they have never felt before?"**

If the answer is no — rewrite it until it does.
