# King Bhishmaka

## Role in the Story
**Father of the Bride** — King of Vidarbha and father of Rukmini and Rukmi.

## Description
King Bhishmaka (also spelled Bhismaka) is the noble and respected ruler of the kingdom of Vidarbha, with his capital at Kundina. He is the father of Rukmini (daughter) and five sons — Rukmi, Rukmaratha, Rukmabahu, Rukmakesa, and Rukmamali.

## Role in Krishna-Rukmini Marriage
- Is sympathetic toward Krishna and personally admires him as a match for Rukmini
- However, he is dominated by the strong-willed Rukmi, his eldest son
- Under Rukmi's pressure, goes along with the arrangement to marry Rukmini to Shishupala
- Is essentially overridden by Rukmi in the marriage decision
- Hosts the grand wedding ceremony at Kundina that ultimately becomes the scene of Rukmini's abduction

## Key Traits
- A good king and a kind father
- Politically weak in the face of Rukmi's dominant personality
- Represents the well-meaning but powerless parental figure caught between political alliances and his children's wishes
- Implicitly approves of Krishna but cannot act against Rukmi's will

## Family
- **Children:** Rukmini (daughter), Rukmi, Rukmaratha, Rukmabahu, Rukmakesa, Rukmamali (five sons)
- **Kingdom:** Vidarbha (capital: Kundina)

## Source
*Bhagavata Purana*, Canto 10, Chapters 52–54
